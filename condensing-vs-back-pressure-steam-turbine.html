Condensing vs back pressure steam turbine

How condensing and back pressure steam turbines differ, and which one fits a site.

Short answer: A condensing steam turbine exhausts into a condenser below atmospheric pressure and turns as much steam energy as possible into electricity. A back pressure steam turbine exhausts steam at a pressure a process can still use, so it produces less power per tonne of steam but supplies heat to the plant at the same time. Extraction turbines combine both by taking process steam out at one or more stages.

Which one should I choose for my site?

If the site needs process steam continuously, a back pressure or extraction turbine uses the steam twice, first for power and then for heat. If only electricity is needed, a condensing turbine gives more power from the same steam.

What is an extraction condensing turbine?

A condensing turbine that also takes steam out at one or more stages for process heat. It can follow changing heat and power demand.

Can a used back pressure turbine be converted to condensing?

Usually not in a simple way. Blading, exhaust casing and the steam path are designed for the original exhaust pressure. Choose the type that matches the site from the start.

Which data does a seller need from a buyer?

Live steam pressure and temperature, steam mass flow, the required exhaust or back pressure, extraction pressures and flows, generator frequency and the site cooling conditions. A used turbine fits only when these values match the boiler and the process.
