Power generation equipment glossary

Short definitions of the terms used when buying and selling used gas turbines, steam turbines and generator sets.

Aeroderivative gas turbine
A gas turbine derived from an aircraft engine, light and quick to start, for example the GE LM2500 and LM6000.
Heavy duty gas turbine
An industrial gas turbine built for continuous operation and long overhaul intervals, for example the GE Frame 9E or Siemens Westinghouse W501.
ISO rating
The output and efficiency of a gas turbine at standard conditions of 15 degrees Celsius, sea level and 60 percent relative humidity. Real output falls at higher ambient temperature and altitude.
Heat rate
The fuel energy needed to produce one unit of electricity, for example in kJ per kWh or Btu per kWh. A lower heat rate means higher efficiency.
Simple cycle
A plant where the gas turbine drives the generator and the exhaust heat leaves through the stack.
Combined cycle (CCGT, CCPP)
A plant where the exhaust heat of the gas turbine makes steam in a heat recovery steam generator and the steam drives a steam turbine for additional power.
HRSG
Heat recovery steam generator. The boiler behind a gas turbine that uses its exhaust heat to make steam.
DLE and DLN
Dry low emissions and dry low NOx. Combustion systems that cut nitrogen oxide emissions without water or steam injection.
Dual fuel
A turbine or engine that can run on two fuels, usually natural gas and diesel or light fuel oil.
Hot gas path inspection
A scheduled inspection of the combustion and turbine parts exposed to the hottest gas.
Major overhaul
The largest scheduled maintenance event, where the turbine or engine is opened completely and worn parts are replaced. Hours since the last major overhaul are a key value for buyers.
Borescope inspection
An inspection of the inside of a turbine or engine through small ports with an optical probe, without opening the casing.
Equivalent operating hours (EOH)
A count that adds operating hours and a weighting for starts, trips and fuel, used by some manufacturers to plan maintenance.
Preservation
Measures that protect a stored unit against corrosion, for example dry air, desiccant and sealed packaging. Buyers of stored units ask for the preservation records.
Condensing steam turbine
A steam turbine that exhausts into a condenser to produce as much power as possible.
Back pressure steam turbine
A steam turbine that exhausts at a pressure a process can still use for heat.
Prime, continuous and standby rating
Generator set ratings for unlimited variable load, unlimited constant load and limited emergency use.
Power barge
A floating power plant on a barge or ship hull, often with gas turbines or engines, that can be moved to a new site.
LOI
Letter of intent. A written statement by the buyer naming the unit, offered price, buyer company, end user, country of installation and payment route. It releases full data and inspection.
POF
Proof of funds. A bank letter confirming that the buyer can pay the purchase price.
NCNDA
Non circumvention and non disclosure agreement between the parties of a deal.
Escrow
A neutral third party that holds the purchase payment until the agreed conditions, for example delivery or transfer of title, are met.
Letter of credit (L/C)
A bank undertaking to pay the seller when the documents named in the credit are presented.
Incoterms (EXW, FOB, CIF, DAP)
Standard trade terms of the International Chamber of Commerce that define where risk and cost pass from seller to buyer.
Exclusive sales mandate
An agreement where an owner gives one broker the sole right to market a unit, so the unit appears with one price and one contact in the market.
